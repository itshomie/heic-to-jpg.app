When PNG is the right output

PNG is a good choice when the next step is editing, quality inspection, or submitting an image where text and edges need to stay crisp. It is lossless, so it does not add another round of JPG compression artifacts to the converted copy.

For ordinary camera photos, PNG files can be much larger than JPG files. If your goal is emailing, marketplace listings, profile photos, or general compatibility, JPG is usually the more practical format. Use PNG when you specifically need lossless output.

How to convert HEIC to PNG

What this HEIC to PNG page is for

This page is focused on one job: turning HEIC photos into PNG files. That matters because people often search for HEIC to PNG when they are not simply trying to share a vacation photo. They may be preparing a screenshot for a support ticket, checking a document image before submitting it, saving a clean reference copy for a designer, or moving a phone photo into software that behaves better with PNG than with HEIC.

The important tradeoff is file size. A PNG copy can preserve visible detail very well, but it usually cannot match HEIC for compression efficiency. A full-resolution iPhone photo that was small as HEIC may become much larger after PNG export. That is expected, not a failure of the converter. PNG is useful when quality and editability are more important than keeping the file compact.

Quality expectations after converting to PNG

PNG does not use the same lossy compression model as JPG, so there is no quality slider for PNG output. The converter decodes the HEIC image and writes a PNG copy from the visible image data. If the original HEIC photo is sharp, the PNG copy should look sharp too. If the original photo is blurry, overexposed, heavily processed, or low resolution, PNG will not repair those problems. It preserves the decoded image; it does not enhance it.

For text-heavy images, inspect the downloaded PNG at actual size before sending it. PNG is often better for edges and lettering than a compressed JPG copy, but the source photo still matters. If the phone camera was too far away from a document or the image was saved from a messaging app at a reduced size, the PNG cannot recover detail that is no longer present.

When not to choose PNG

Do not choose PNG just because it sounds higher quality. For normal camera photos, JPG is usually easier to upload, email, and store. A PNG version of a photo with many colors, gradients, shadows, and texture can become large enough that forms reject it or email clients compress it again. If the destination asks for a photo upload and accepts JPG, JPG is normally the better practical choice.

PNG is also not a metadata-preservation format for every HEIC feature. The exported file is intended to be a compatible image copy. HEIC-specific information such as Live Photo motion, depth data, burst details, and some camera metadata may not be present in the PNG. Keep the original HEIC file when those details matter, and use the PNG as the working or upload copy.

HEIC to PNG checklist

  • Use PNG when you need lossless output for review, editing, screenshots, or document-like images.
  • Use JPG instead when the goal is broad photo compatibility with smaller file sizes.
  • Expect PNG files to be larger than the original HEIC files, especially for full-resolution photos.
  • Download one converted PNG and inspect it before processing a large batch.
  • Keep the original HEIC files if you may need metadata or Apple-specific photo features later.
