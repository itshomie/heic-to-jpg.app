Why people still need JPG copies

HEIC is a good camera format, especially on iPhone, because it keeps image files small. The problem usually appears later: a government form rejects the upload, an older editor cannot open the file, or a marketplace asks for JPG. This page is built for that moment: make a compatible JPG copy and keep the original HEIC file untouched.

What happens during conversion

The converter reads the HEIC image in your browser, draws it into a local image canvas, and exports a new JPG file using the quality setting you choose. Because JPG does not keep every HEIC feature, metadata such as Live Photo data, depth information, and some camera details may not be preserved in the exported file.

A practical HEIC to JPG guide

Most conversions are straightforward, but the right setting depends on where the image is going. These notes are written for normal photo workflows rather than lab-perfect image processing.

For forms and ID uploads

Use JPG quality around 85-90%. It usually keeps text, faces, and document edges readable while avoiding oversized files that upload forms may reject.

For family photos and printing

Keep quality near 95-100% if the photo will be edited again or printed. The file will be larger, but it leaves more detail in skies, skin tones, and shadows.

For email and support tickets

A lower quality setting can be fine when the goal is simply to show what happened. Check the preview and download one file first if the photo contains small text.

For batches from iPhone

Select multiple HEIC files at once, let the results finish, then use “Download all as ZIP” so the browser saves the JPG copies together.

HEIC to JPG FAQ

What is HEIC?

HEIC is an image format commonly used by iPhone and iPad cameras. It keeps photos small while preserving quality. It is common on Apple devices, but many forms, websites, and older apps still expect JPG.

Is this HEIC to JPG converter really free?

Yes. The converter is free to use and does not require an account.

Are my photos uploaded to your server?

No. The converter processes files locally in your browser. We do not receive the photos you choose, and the resulting JPG files are created on your device.

Will I lose quality when converting HEIC to JPG?

JPG uses compression, so very low quality settings can reduce detail. Keep the slider near 90% or higher for everyday photos, and higher if you plan to edit or print the JPG.

Which browsers are supported?

Modern versions of Chrome, Safari, Edge, and Firefox are supported when JavaScript, WebAssembly, local file access, and downloads are available.

Can I convert several HEIC files at once?

Yes. Select multiple HEIC or HEIF files, wait for the results, then download each JPG or save the full batch as a ZIP archive.

Does the JPG keep the original photo metadata?

The exported JPG focuses on image compatibility. Some metadata and HEIC-specific features may not be included, so keep your original HEIC files if those details matter.
