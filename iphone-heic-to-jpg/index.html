Why iPhone photos are HEIC

Apple devices use HEIC because it stores high-quality photos efficiently. That is useful for saving space in Photos and iCloud, but it can become a problem outside the Apple ecosystem. Some upload forms, older Windows apps, editors, support portals, and marketplaces still expect JPG or JPEG.

How to convert iPhone HEIC photos to JPG

  1. Move the HEIC photos to your computer, or choose them from a folder synced from iCloud.
  2. Open the HEIC to JPG converter and select the files.
  3. Use 90% JPG quality for normal sharing and uploads.
  4. Download the JPG copies and keep the original HEIC files if the photos matter.

Should you turn off HEIC on iPhone?

You can change camera settings on the phone, but it is not always necessary. HEIC is efficient for storage, and conversion is only needed when another service rejects the file. A practical workflow is to keep taking HEIC photos, then make JPG copies only for the files you need to send or upload.

Move iPhone photos to the converter

On a Mac, AirDrop and Photos export are common ways to get the HEIC files into a folder. On Windows, iCloud Photos or a direct phone transfer may create a folder of HEIC images. Once the files are visible in Finder or File Explorer, the browser can read the selected files and create JPG copies locally.

If you are downloading from iCloud.com, check whether the download is the original HEIC or an already converted image. Different export choices can change the file type. If the file name ends in .heic or .heif, this converter is the right page. If the file is already .jpg or .jpeg, conversion is not needed.

Use JPG copies for forms and sharing

Many upload forms use simple extension checks. They may reject an iPhone HEIC photo even when the image is perfectly valid. A JPG copy avoids that problem because the format is recognized almost everywhere. It is also easier to preview in older software, send to people on mixed devices, and attach to forms that were built before HEIC became common.

For forms, start around 90% JPG quality. For documents, receipts, or IDs, use a higher setting so small text and edges remain readable. For casual sharing, a slightly lower setting can be enough. The right setting depends on the destination, not the fact that the source came from an iPhone.

Keep originals when photo details matter

iPhone photos can contain more than the visible image. Depending on the photo and settings, the original may include camera metadata, location data, depth information, or Live Photo motion. A JPG conversion is meant for compatibility and may not preserve every detail. That is why the safest workflow is to keep the HEIC original and use the JPG as a copy.

This is especially important for important family photos, legal records, product archives, and professional work. Once you know the JPG has been accepted by the website or person who needs it, you can keep both files: HEIC for storage and JPG for compatibility.
